A customizable platform for FPGA adaptation & resilience

At advanced nodes, every device is different.

Variation and latent defects force sign-off to guardband every device for the slowest silicon at the worst-case corner. That conservatism costs performance and energy, and it still misses the defects that fail in the field. Fluid Silicon measures every device at speed, in-system, exposes those defects and keeps each device inside its measured margin.

In the field

Timing failures sign-off never sees.

One cause: variation and latent defects make each device's real margin different, and nothing measures it after sign-off. That margin varies across the die and shrinks with temperature and age. Monitoring is read-only; tuning and repair are separate steps you enable.

How it works

Measure every device at speed. Keep it inside its margin.

A device is characterized once, against the slowest silicon at the worst-case corner, and runs on that assumption for life. Fluid Silicon adds a lightweight health layer alongside your design that measures setup slack in-system, at operating frequency, for as long as the device runs.

{{icon:monitor}}

Measured in-system

Setup slack on individual LUTs, carry chains and the routing between them, while your design runs. A full sweep takes about a second; each link is resolved to under 20 ps.

{{icon:model}}

Modeled per device

An aging curve fitted to each device's temperature history, with a time-to-threshold for every element on the die.

{{icon:tune}}

Held within measured margin

Vccint and clock follow the margin each device has measured, never past it, in maintenance windows you schedule, re-measured after every change.

{{icon:repair}}

Repaired only when needed

Nothing in your design moves until an element degrades toward your threshold. Then only that path is re-placed onto healthy resources, through your change process.

Where this goes

FPGAs are where we start.

A device that measures its own timing belongs wherever silicon has to be trusted. FPGAs are reprogrammable, so the health layer can be added to devices already in service, with no new silicon. That makes them the first step.

1

Today: FPGAs, read-only

Per-element measurement on AMD and Altera devices, with evaluations that start on a few of your boards.

2

Next: the full loop on every device

Each device monitored, modeled, tuned and, when needed, repaired on its own measurement: on the bench, in a vehicle, in a cabinet or in a data center.

3

After that: beyond FPGAs

The same per-element measurement for other silicon that has to run for years, so no system fails because a part ran out of margin unnoticed.
