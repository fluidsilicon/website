    Four layers, one data path

    Agents in the fabric. A flow that puts them there. Applications that act on what comes back. Nothing here asks you to redesign anything.

    Layer 01

    Fabric agents

    Soft-IP blocks placed in fabric your design was not using. Each one measures something specific and reports it as a number, not a flag.

    • Margin agents — how much slack a representative path has right now, at speed
    • Delay agents — drift in fabric propagation as voltage, temperature and age move
    • Thermal agents — junction temperature per region rather than per package
    • Rail agents — droop and ripple on the core supply, sampled where it matters
    Agent placement · illustrative

    Layer 02

    Integration and implementation

    Agents go in after synthesis, as part of your existing implementation run. Nothing upstream of that changes.

    Step 1 · Plan

    Coverage from the netlist

    The planner reads your synthesised netlist and floorplan, then proposes agent placement to cover the regions and paths that actually matter. You approve it or edit it.

    Step 2 · Insert

    Into the implementation run

    Insertion happens inside your normal place-and-route flow, in leftover fabric, with constraints that keep your critical paths untouched. Your RTL is not modified.

    Step 3 · Verify

    Sign-off you can read

    A report shows exactly what was added, what it cost in area and power, and confirms timing closed with the same margin you had before.

    Layer 03

    Production applications

    On the tester, in the time budget you already have.

    • Margin readout as a test insertion, feeding your existing data pipeline
    • Outlier detection across the lot, not a fixed threshold per device
    • Per-part records keyed to serial number, so field returns trace back

    Layer 04

    In-field applications

    After the board ships, running for as long as it does.

    • Closed-loop voltage and frequency scaling with a hardware safety net
    • Fleet dashboards that rank devices by remaining margin
    • Alerts your firmware can catch in microseconds, not seconds

    Practical questions

    The three things you are about to ask

    Footprint

    What does it cost me in fabric?

    A typical deployment lands in low single-digit percent of LUTs and flip-flops, placed in regions your design left empty. The implementation report gives you the exact number for your design before you commit to it.

    Flow

    Does it fit my toolchain?

    Insertion runs inside the vendor implementation flows your team already uses, driven by scripts you can read and check into your repo alongside everything else. No parallel flow to maintain.

    Data

    Where does the telemetry go?

    Wherever you say. Our cloud, your VPC, or entirely on-prem with no egress. The applications are the same in all three; only the deployment target changes.
