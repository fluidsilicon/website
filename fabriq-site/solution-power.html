Power and thermal

Scale the core rail against measured margin instead of a datasheet worst case. Watts come out. The clock stays where it is.

The problem

Why this costs you

Voltage is set once, at design time, for the weakest part you might ever ship, at the hottest corner it might ever see. Every other part in the fleet — which is nearly all of them — runs with the rail higher than it needs to be, converting the difference into heat.

Dropping the rail is easy. Dropping it and knowing you are still safe is the part that needs a measurement.

What you get

What changes

  • Closed-loop voltage scaling driven by margin measured on that specific part, right now
  • A hardware failure-avoidance layer underneath: if margin drops, the rail comes back up before a path does
  • Per-region junction temperature, so you find the hot spot instead of the hot chassis
  • Rack-level power numbers you can hand to whoever signs the electricity bill
